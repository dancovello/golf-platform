# Marketing site tone of voice

## The one rule

**Sell the feeling of a club website that looks after itself, not the list of things it can do.**

Features are proof, never the pitch. Mention a block, setting or tool only to explain *why* it's easy or *why* it always looks right.

| Feature-led (don't) | Experience-led (do) |
|---|---|
| 30+ customisable blocks including hero banners, galleries and event listings. | Every block is designed to sit well next to every other. Rearrange, restyle, add your photos. It still looks like a designer built it. |
| Role-based permissions and draft workflows. | Invite your committee. Give each person only the access they need. |
| Live preview editor. | Type on the left. See it on your site on the right. |

## Identity

A golf club website platform designed and built by Dan, a senior product designer. Every block was designed to fit every other block, so a club can make the site its own and it still looks professional. We are small, specific and close to our clubs. That is a strength: say it plainly.

## Core belief

**Your club is better than your website.**

A club's website is the first impression for most visitors. If it lets them down, they never see the course, the restaurant, the bar or the people. The website should be a tool that sells the club, not a job that drains the secretary.

## Audience

**Primary: the club manager or secretary.** Doing five jobs. Not a web person. Has inherited a site that's slow to update, looks dated, or depends on "the member who's good with computers". Wants it sorted without it becoming another project.

**Secondary: the committee and owners they have to convince.** Cares about cost, risk, reputation and what happens if things go wrong.

Write so the primary reader feels understood, and leaves with the argument to win over the second. Every page should hand them a line they could repeat at a committee meeting.

## Voice traits

| We are | Sounds like | Not |
|---|---|---|
| **Assured** | Calm certainty. A good head pro, not a salesman. | Shouty, hype, "best ever", exclamation marks |
| **Warm** | We like clubs and what happens inside them. | Techy, platform-speak, cold SaaS |
| **Plain-spoken** | Short sentences. Real outcomes. Everyday words. | Jargon, acronyms, feature names as headlines |
| **Quietly premium** | Restraint. Taste shows in what we leave out. | Golf puns, gimmicks, clutter |
| **Honest** | Specific claims we can prove. Roadmap labelled as roadmap. | Vague superlatives, invented numbers, implied features |

## Message pillars

Every page leads with one of these. Most sections should map to one.

1. **Time back.** Updates take minutes, not a call to an agency. More time with members, less time in the admin.
2. **Confidence in every change.** Make it yours, and it still looks great. Freedom with guardrails.
3. **First impressions.** The website is the clubhouse door. Show visitors who you are before they decide to leave.
4. **Yours, not your neighbour's.** No rigid template that makes every club look the same. Your colours, your photos, your layout.

## Writing rules

**Open with the outcome, not the windup.** First line of any page or section is a clear benefit or a sharp truth. Never a definition. Never "Welcome to…" or "Have you ever…".

**Short sentences, short paragraphs, lots of air.** One idea per paragraph. Aim for under 20 words per sentence. Headlines under 8 words where possible.

**Bold the takeaway, not the setup.** One or two bold phrases per paragraph at most. A reader who scans only the bold should get the argument.

**Have a point of view.** "Most club websites are hard to update and harder to love" beats "Some websites may present challenges."

**Let specifics do the bragging.** "Change your green fees in under a minute" beats "Incredibly easy to use". Specifics only if they're true (see Honesty).

**Talk to one person.** "You" and "your club". "We" for us. Never "users", "customers" or "stakeholders" in copy.

**Say what it is.** One sentence, then move on. Never define the product by a run of things it isn't.

**Every sentence earns its place.** If deleting a line makes the section better, delete it.

## Guardrails

**Stakes without shame.** We can say an old website lets a club down. We never mock the club, their current site or whoever built it. Many readers built or inherited it themselves. The website is the problem, never the person.

**Never punch down.** No "hacked together", "amateur", "cowboy" or digs at agencies, volunteers or competitors. If we need a foil, it's bad *structures* (rigid templates, slow updates, sites only one person can edit), not bad people.

**Show "better", don't claim it.** No "best", "number one", "better than anyone", "industry-leading". Prove difference with something checkable: public prices, a live demo, our own top-100 audit.

**Confident, not performative.** No manifesto energy, triple anaphora, or bumper-sticker closers.

**Golf flavour, not golf puns.** Use real club language (green fees, course status, the clubhouse, the committee, the first tee) because it shows we know the world. No "fore", "hole-in-one", "below par", "tee up", "in the swing".

## Honesty rules (from doc 18, non-negotiable)

1. Present tense only for things that work in production today.
2. Anything not live says **Coming next** with a season, never a date we can't hit.
3. No numbers without a source we can show and a date measured.
4. No absolutes ("every", "always", "never breaks", "automatically") unless literally true.
5. "We" is fine. Never imply staff, offices or departments that don't exist.
6. Every factual claim has a row in the claims register before it ships.

Test for every line: **if a club secretary screenshotted it and sent it back in six months, could we point to it working?**

## Vocabulary

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| your club, your site | tenant, instance, account |
| club, members, visitors | users, customers, end users |
| blocks | components, modules, widgets |
| make it yours | customise, configure |
| update, change, publish | manage, administer, deploy |
| minutes | seamless, effortless |
| looks professional, looks right | pixel-perfect, stunning |
| your committee, your team | stakeholders, admins |
| coming next | coming soon, on the roadmap, in development |
| club log in | sign in to the platform, dashboard |
| switching to us | migration, onboarding |

## Banned words and phrases

- solution, platform (in headlines), leverage, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, revolutionary, game-changer
- seamless, effortless, world-class, best-in-class, industry-leading, all-in-one
- "Welcome to…", "In today's digital world…", "We're excited to announce…"
- "Don't miss out!", "Limited spaces!" (unless the count is real), "Act now"
- "Look no further", "Take your club to the next level", "Elevate your…"
- "If this resonates, you're in the right place"
- Triple anaphora for drama ("No X. No Y. No Z.")
- Exclamation marks in headlines
- Any golf pun

## Channel rules

| Surface | Rules |
|---|---|
| **Headlines** | Under 8 words. Outcome first. No questions unless the answer is obvious and in our favour. |
| **Subheads** | One sentence. Explains the headline in plain terms. Says what's live. |
| **Body** | Max 3 short paragraphs per section. Scannable. |
| **Buttons** | Verb plus outcome: "Book a demo", "See your club on it", "Apply for a founding place". Never "Submit", "Learn more", "Click here". |
| **Feature cards** | Benefit as the title, one line of how. Availability badge if not live. |
| **Pricing** | Price, VAT treatment and term together. Plain answers to committee questions. |
| **FAQs** | Answer in the first sentence. Yes or no first where possible. |
| **Forms and errors** | Human and specific: "We'll send your homepage by Thursday 23 October." Never blame the reader. |
| **Meta descriptions** | One benefit, one proof, under 155 characters. |

## Reference examples

These set the standard. New copy should sound like it belongs next to them.

**Hero**
> **Your club is better than your website.**
> A golf club website your team can update in minutes, and that still looks professional when they're done.

**Builder section**
> **Make it yours. It'll still look right.**
> Every block was designed to fit every other block. Change the layout, add your colours and photos, move things around. The design holds, so your site never looks thrown together.

**First impressions section**
> **Most visitors meet your club online first.**
> If the website lets them down, they never see the course, the food, the bar or the people behind the desk. Give them a reason to come and find out.

**Time section**
> **Course closed? Tell everyone before the phone rings.**
> Post a course update from your phone in under a minute. Members see it the next time they visit.
> *(Only once course status and mobile upload are tested — see doc 18.)*

**Founding clubs**
> **Ten clubs help shape what we build next.**
> Founding clubs get a lower price for as long as they stay with us, first access to new features, and a direct line to the person building it.

## Checklist before any copy ships

- [ ] Opens with an outcome, not a windup
- [ ] Leads with one message pillar
- [ ] Sounds assured, warm, plain, quietly premium
- [ ] Features appear only as proof of an experience
- [ ] Uses preferred vocabulary; no banned words or phrases
- [ ] No golf puns
- [ ] No shaming the club or punching down at anyone
- [ ] "Better" is shown with something checkable, not claimed
- [ ] Every factual claim is live, sourced and in the claims register
- [ ] Anything not live is badged Coming next with a season
- [ ] Bold phrases carry the argument for scanners
- [ ] Every sentence earns its place
